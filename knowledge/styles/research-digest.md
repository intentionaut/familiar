# Style: Research digest

A weekly or fortnightly summary of what changed in a field. The interview
becomes a source-selection pass. The draft is a curated list, not an essay.

## Positioning

```
Language: en
Tone: precise, opinionated, brief
Audience: practitioners who need to know what changed and why it matters
Cadence: weekly or fortnightly
```

## Voice

Short sentences. Lead with the finding, not the paper. Every item answers
three questions: what did they find, why does it matter, what should I do
about it? No hedging ("interesting results"). Say "this changes X" or "this
does not change Y" and cite the source.

## Slot shapes

- **Issue**: 8-12 items, each 2-4 sentences, grouped by theme
- **Social**: one post per item, each a standalone takeaway
- **No long repurpose**: the digest IS the long form

## What to change

- `positioning.md`: set the field, the audience level, and what counts as
  worth including
- `voice-guide.md`: add the "no hedging" rule and the three-question test
- `social-schedule.md`: the social stage works, but the cadence is per-item
  not per-essay
- Create a `knowledge/sources.md` with the feeds, journals, and people you
  track. The interview stage reads this instead of asking "what are you
  thinking about?"

## Adapting the pipeline

1. **Replace `interview` with `bring`**: the source list is your notes. Use
   `familiar bring sources.md` to start from what you tracked this week.
2. **`outline` becomes grouping**: instead of three structures, it offers
   three ways to group the items (by theme, by importance, by audience).
3. **`draft` becomes curation**: it writes the intro and the item cards,
   leaving `[NEEDS SOURCE]` brackets where a link or citation is missing.
4. **`dev-edit` becomes fact-check**: it flags claims without sources and
   items that do not answer the three questions.
5. **Skip `line-edit` or keep it short**: the mechanical pass matters less
   when each item is 2-4 sentences.
