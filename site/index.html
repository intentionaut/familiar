Familiar

A companion for your newsletter.

You finish something good and then cannot say how you did it. The reasons were obvious in June and they are gone by September. Familiar takes you through a series of gates, each one asking for the part only you can give, until what you know is written down in your own words and ready to send.

How a piece moves

The stopping is the point.

A model left to run from one end to the other gives you something smooth and slightly wrong. Each step inherits the last step's small errors and there is nobody standing there to catch them. So Familiar stops, every time, and the thing it wants next is a decision only you can make.

You can walk back through it too. Return to the interview and your notes grow. Rework one section and the rest is left where it was. The piece waits in a folder between gates, which some weeks is an hour and other weeks is a fortnight.

01Interview

One question at a time, starting with what happened to you. You end with a thesis in one sentence, the stakes, an evidence list, and the lines worth building on.familiar interview "why our churn model kept lying"

02Outline

Three shapes that are genuinely different, each with where your best line lands and one way it could fail. You pick, merge or veto. It will not draft until you have.familiar outline

03Draft

About eighty percent of a draft you can polish, already in your voice with your words. Where it would have invented a number or a quote, it leaves a bracket and says what it needed.familiar draft

04Dev edit

The demanding, loyal editor. Where the spark is, whether the argument holds, what has to move, each with an exact rewrite. A report you work through yourself. Your draft is untouched.familiar dev-edit

05Line edit

The boring pass, run last. The patterns that give a model away, your house spelling and dash rule, reading ease. When it cannot tell a tell from your dry wit, it says so.familiar line-edit

06Repurpose

One finished piece, two ways to use it again. Short is a week of posts. Long is a companion piece for somewhere else: it reads what you already have and hands you back to the interview.familiar repurpose

07Social

A week of posts on your own cadence. One pool of candidates, you pick per channel, exact send times. It ends at copy you have approved, and goes no further.familiar social next

08Publish

The posts you approved, into your scheduler. It builds each link and counts the finished post before it schedules, because a post that fitted in the draft stops fitting once the tracking is on the end of it. Connect nothing and you get a table to paste.familiar publish

09Learn

Hand it the issue you sent next to the draft it gave you. The edits you made twice become rules, with the sentence each came from. Or point it at your back catalogue and let it draft your voice files from evidence.familiar learn diff pieces/2026-09-03-churn

Before you start

The first hour is mostly you talking.

Familiar cannot draft in your voice until it has one to work from. If you have published before, point the learn stage at that folder and it will read your writing and draft the voice files from evidence, with counts rather than adjectives, and you accept or reject each section. If you have not published, you answer two short files instead. Either way this happens before your first draft, not after you are disappointed by one.

Then the interview, which is the part people try to skip. It asks one question at a time and it wants real answers, because your answers are the material. Ten or fifteen honest minutes here is the difference between a piece that sounds like you and a piece that sounds like a competent stranger who read your notes.

What you should not expect is a finished piece. The draft stage gets you most of the way in your own words, and the edit stages hand back a report you work through yourself. The last stretch is yours. That is the design, not a limitation being apologised for.
