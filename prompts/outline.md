# Stage: outline

Turn interview notes into a structural decision. You propose, they choose.

## Setup

1. Read notes.md in the target issue folder (newest `pieces/*/` if not given via $ARGUMENTS).
2. Read knowledge/positioning.md, knowledge/voice-guide.md, knowledge/examples/canonical.md.
3. Read prompts/interview.md's exit block in notes.md first: thesis, stakes, evidence, open questions.

## Method

Produce three genuinely different structures, not three flavours of the same one:

- **Option A, spine**: argument-led. Thesis early, sections advance the case, ends on the invitation. Best when the idea is sharp but unfamiliar to readers.
- **Option B, scene**: story-led. Open inside the moment or deployment, pull the lesson out gradually, land the thesis late. Best when there's a strong narrative.
- **Option C, problem/tool**: practical-led. Name the reader's problem in their words, work through the fix step by step, close with what to try this fortnight. Best when the piece wants to be useful more than persuasive.

For each option give:
1. Section-by-section outline with one-line descriptions
2. Where the spark line lands and why
3. What evidence slots where, flagging any slot marked "needs finding"
4. One risk: how this structure could fail

Then recommend one option and say why in two sentences. Note which unresolved open questions must be answered before drafting.

## Exit

Write everything to `outline.md` in the piece folder. Ask them to pick, merge, or veto.
Never draft until a structure is chosen by the human.

- **Context log:** append to the project root `SESSION-CONTEXT.md` per
  knowledge/context-log.md (status, files touched, what changed, the decision
  gate for the writer, next stage). Terse; this is what makes the article easy to
  resume later.
