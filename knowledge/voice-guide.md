# Voice guide

How you write. Familiar reads this before drafting or editing anything, and
treats `examples/canonical.md` as the proof. Filled 2026-09-13 by `learn`
ingest from your LinkedIn share export: 1,681 posts, 2013-03 to 2026-09.
492 automated cross-posts (IFTTT, Buffer, "I just finished reading") were
excluded before counting, leaving 920 posts and 26,843 words. Every rule
below carries its count.

## Register

Who is the piece written to, and from where?

- One reader, a peer, spoken to across a table. Direct address is constant:
  "you/your" appears 22.8 times per 1,000 words across the corpus, 24.0 in
  2022-2026.
- Personal stakes appear in the first line, not the third paragraph. 19 of
  110 long posts (300+ characters) open with "I"; the rest open with a scene
  or a flat claim (see Moves). Nobody is warmed up first.
- Warmth is specific, not general. When you thank people you name them and
  say what they did: Kate Leto, Matt LeMay, Martin Eriksson, Emily Tate, each
  with a sentence of evidence (2026-06-28). "I love Matt LeMay and everything
  he writes." (2026-09-09) is the whole post.
- Too much warmth is the 2019-2021 register: exclamation marks ran 19.2 per
  1,000 words then, against 1.7 in 2025-2026. Current writing almost never
  exclaims.

## Sentence craft

- Median sentence is 14 words in current writing (2022-2026); 33% of
  sentences run under 12 words. Long sentences exist but earn their place.
- Fragments are deliberate and stay: 9% of all sentences are three words or
  fewer (140 of 1,625). "Not years. Days." (2026-09-04). "Are we surprised?
  No." (2026-08-22). The line edit leaves these alone.
- Punctuation habits, counted across 26,843 words:
  - Em dashes: 44 total (1.64 per 1,000 words), but 30 of them sit in one
    August-September 2025 cluster of long analytical posts, and the 13 posts
    of 2026 contain none. Treat the em dash as not your default; recast with
    a comma, colon or full stop.
  - Semicolons: 11 total (0.41 per 1,000 words). You essentially do not use
    them.
  - Exclamation marks: declining steeply by era (19.2 per 1,000 in 2019-2021,
    3.2 in 2022-2026, 1.7 in 2025-2026). One per piece at most.
  - Question marks: rising (6.2 per 1,000 overall; 39% of 2025-2026 posts
    contain a question). Questions are a current habit, especially at the
    close.
- Rhythm: it breathes. A long sentence is followed by a short one, often a
  fragment. "The building is the fast part." (2026-09-04) lands right after
  the longest sentence in its post.
- Contractions are routine (13.5 per 1,000 words): "it's", "you're", "I've".
  Never expand them.
- Lists carry concrete nouns and real numbers, not abstractions: "275 SaaS
  applications on average", "£21M annually wasted" (2025-09-08).

## Moves that make it yours

1. Open with one flat line: a scene or a claim, no preamble. "The prototype
   has been two sprints away for four months." (2026-08-31). "You can ship
   something in a weekend now. The building is the fast part." (2026-09-04).
   "Everyone's analysing Atlassian's $610M browser acquisition wrong."
   (2025-09-08).
2. Ground the argument in a number or a named source early: "the Wall Street
   Journal reports" (2026-08-20); "Gartner just predicted that 40% of
   agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027" (2025-08-14). 25 posts cite
   a percentage; 22 name a major publication.
3. Correct yourself on the page: "Not X. Y." or "Not because X. Because Y."
   "Not years. Days." (2026-09-04). "Not because anyone is slow. Because
   everyone who could build it is committed to this quarter" (2026-08-31).
4. Say the uncomfortable thing plainly, without softening. "I'd like to feel
   more optimistic but I don't see how." (2026-03-31). "Sometimes the honest
   answer is that you should not build it." (2026-08-31).
5. Headings, when a post has them, are plain statements or short questions,
   sentence case. The 2025 analytical posts sometimes use ALL-CAPS subheads
   ("THE HIDDEN PRODUCTIVITY CRISIS", 2025-09-08); that is the exception
   cluster, not the house style.
6. End by handing the piece to the reader: a question or an invitation. 19
   posts end on a question mark; "let me know" appears 7 times. "If the thing
   you can nearly describe has been sitting in a document since spring, that
   is a conversation I want to have." (2026-08-31). "Comments welcome!"
   (2026-08-28).

## Authority

You show, you do not tell. No post in the current era opens with a job title
or a years-of-experience claim; credibility comes from one precise scene or
number written as a participant. The 2026-08-31 consulting post establishes
authority entirely through the work: "Your repo, your stack, your data."

- Show: a scene, a number, a named source, a thing you built.
- Never: resume drops, "as a [title]", writing about other people's work
  from above. The 2022-2024 listicle era did this ("insider tips",
  "uncommon advice", 2023-06-20); it is the register you have since dropped.

## Hard noes

The list the line edit will enforce without mercy. Add to it every time you
correct the same thing twice.

- "Tell the story" and "a familiar moment" (banned in your 2026-09-13 review
  sessions; same family: "a familiar scene", "a familiar feeling"). Note:
  "tell the story" appears once in the corpus, in your own 2026-09-04 post.
  The ban stands; the line edit flags it anyway.
- Hype vocabulary. These cluster in 2022-2024 and vanish in 2025-2026, which
  is as close to a ban as evidence gets: "journey" (12 uses in 2022-2024, 1
  since), "unlock" (11, then 0), "crucial" (6, then 0), "foster" (5, then 0),
  "excited to" (8, then 0), "navigate" the complexities (7, then 1),
  "game-changer" (4, then 1). Also the standing list in
  `knowledge/style-rules.md`.
- Emoji as decoration. Emoji rise from 0% of posts (2013-2018) to 33%
  (2025-2026), but only 1 of the 13 posts in 2026 uses any. Emoji bullets
  and emoji-bracketed titles ("🌟 ... 🌟") are the 2021-2025 listicle habit,
  not your current voice.
- Hashtag clouds. 39 hashtags per 1,000 words in 2019-2021, 11.6 in
  2025-2026, and none in the 2026 essay posts.
- "In today's [fast-paced / AI-driven] world" openings: present in the
  2025 analytical cluster ("In today's AI-driven world", 2025-09-05), absent
  everywhere since.
- Marketing rhythm: "not just X, but Y" still appears 4 times in 2025-2026.
  Watch item, not yet a ban; flag every instance for a decision.
- "Transform" and its derivatives: 8 uses in 2025-2026. Watch item; flag
  unless literal.
- American spellings. British is your dominant convention (organis- 19 vs
  organiz- 9; colour 4 vs color 0; analys- 8 vs analyz- 0) but slips exist
  (center 7 vs centre 4). The line edit fixes to British.
- Anything that would not be said aloud to a friend. The test every rule
  above is approximating.
