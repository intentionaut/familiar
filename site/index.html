Familiar

Your voice, written down.

Writing about your own work is hard for a specific reason. The good part is the part you stopped noticing.

Familiar asks while the work is still warm. It interviews you, proposes structures, drafts in your voice, and hands back an editor's report instead of a rewrite. Every stage stops and waits for you.

You       A Tuesday. The model said 4% monthly churn and

What you get

A draft in your voice, not a rewrite.

Familiar cannot write in your voice until it has one. Fill in two files: what the publication is, and how you write. Or point it at your back catalogue and let it draft them from evidence.

Then the interview, which is the part people try to skip. Ten or fifteen honest minutes of one question at a time, and you have a thesis, stakes, and the lines worth building on.

The draft gets you most of the way in your own words. Where it would have invented a number or a quote, it leaves a bracket. The edits come back as a report you work through yourself. The last stretch is yours, and that is deliberate.

Posts get the same treatment, compressed. A quality pass catches the things that give a model away, and you approve each post before it goes anywhere.

How it works

Three ways in. The rest is a conversation.

Three commands, because there are three ways a writing session starts: you are beginning something, picking something back up, or looking for what to write about. Everything after that is agent-routed: tell the agent what you have and it picks the right stage.

  1. /board

    See everything in flight

    Your pieces by state, what each one needs next, what you cut and can reuse. A board you refresh in one command.

  2. /new-piece

    Start something

    Scaffolds the folder, then goes straight into the interview: one question at a time until the idea has a thesis and the stakes are on the page.

  3. /harvest

    When you do not know what to write about

    Reads your build logs across projects and extracts recurring themes, growth signals, and ready-to-go topics.

The rest of the pipeline

Outline, draft, dev edit, line edit, finalise, social, repurpose, publish, learn, reflect, log, inspire. The agent routes on what you say: "I have a draft" starts dev edit. "turn this into posts" starts social. "I have a build log" starts case study.

Pieces, not templates

A piece is a folder of files.

Every piece lives in pieces/YYYY-MM-DD-slug/. Notes, drafts, outlines, edits, social posts, the context log. Put it down for a fortnight and it is exactly where you left it, with a note telling you what it was waiting on.

You can go backwards as easily as forwards. Return to the interview and your notes grow. Rework one section and the rest stays where it was. Nothing you decided earlier gets overwritten by something later.

When a piece ships, the board knows. When you cut something good, it surfaces as work rather than being lost in a file nobody opens.
