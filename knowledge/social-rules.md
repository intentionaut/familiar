# Social rules (the pass a short post gets)

`style-rules.md` is written for a piece. It catches the words. A post that
passes every rule in it can still be one you would not sign your name to,
because the ways a short post fails are not mechanical. It has two ideas welded
together. It cannot be understood by someone who has not read the piece. It
stops on a link instead of a thought. It repeats what you posted on Monday.

None of those show up as a flagged word, so this file exists. Run it **with**
`style-rules.md`, never instead of it.

## The four tests

Every post gets all four, and they run in this order because an earlier failure
makes a later one meaningless. There is no point tightening the ending of a post
that is two posts.

### 1. One post, one viewpoint

A post says one thing. Name the thing in a sentence, out loud, before judging
anything else about the post. If naming it needs an "and", the post is two
posts and the tests stop here.

The failure is easiest to make when a post is assembled rather than written:
built from two candidates, updated after the facts changed, or stretched to
cover a second thing the writer wanted in. The seams do not read as seams. They
read as a post that is somehow flat.

Symptoms, any one of which is enough to fail the test:

- More than one move in it. An announcement, then a story, then an argument,
  then a product mention is four.
- A paragraph that could be cut with nothing else changing.
- The opening promises one thing and the ending delivers another.
- You cannot say which sentence the post exists for.

**A post that fails this test does not get a list of fixes.** See "When a post
has no single viewpoint" below.

### 2. It stands on its own

The reader has not read the piece, does not know what you shipped, and is not
going to click first. Everything the post needs is in the post.

- No pronoun without its noun in the same post. "It changed how I work" is not
  a post if "it" is only named in the newsletter.
- No inside reference: a project name, a tool, a person, a previous post,
  carried in without a clause that says what it is.
- Only the slot whose job is to point at the piece may lead with the
  publication. Everywhere else, opening on your own admin spends the first line,
  which is the only line most people read, on something that is about you rather
  than about them.

### 3. It ends on a thought

The last line is the one that gets remembered or ignored, and it is where the
short forms go wrong most reliably.

- A link is not an ending. Put it after the ending.
- Neither is a summary of what the post just said.
- Neither is "read the full thing", in any of its costumes.
- A question at the end is an ending only when you would want the answers. A
  question asked to prompt replies is engagement bait and reads as one.

The test: cut the last line. If the post is better, that line was furniture.

### 4. The week is not the same post five times

Posts are approved one at a time and read as a set. This test runs across every
post going out in the same window, on the same channel, and it is the one that
cannot be run on a single post in isolation.

Fail on any of:

- Two posts sharing a paragraph, a sentence, or an image, in any wording close
  enough that a reader who saw both would notice.
- Two posts opening the same way, structurally. Two that open on a flat
  first-person declaration are the same opening even with different words.
- Two making the same argument from different angles, unless that is a
  deliberate run and the writer said so.
- The same verbal tic in more than one post. A phrase that is fine once is a
  habit at twice and a signature at three times.

## When a post has no single viewpoint

Do not hand back a list of fixes. A post with two ideas in it is not a post with
nine problems, and nine problems is the wrong conversation: the writer ends up
patching sentences in something that should not have been assembled.

Offer viewpoints instead, as a pick, per AGENTS.md, "Offering options, and
recording the pick":

- **Two to four**, each one drawn from material the post or its source already
  contains. Never invented from outside it.
- **Fully written, never described.** "The angle about timing" is not an option.
  The opening line, plus one line on where the post goes from there, is.
- `Buys:` and `Costs:` on each.
- **Always an escape.** "Or something else, if none of these is it."
- One letter is a complete answer.

Then, at most two questions before writing it, and they ask for judgement, never
for recall. Do not ask the writer what happened: that is in the piece, the notes
or the record, and going to get it is your job. Ask what they think, what they
would say to one person about it, what they want the reader left holding.

Ask them one at a time, and write the post from their answers in their words
wherever their words will carry it.

Record the pick as an options block with `Chosen` and `Because`, in the file the
posts live in. `Because` is the line `learn decisions` reads, so it is the
writer's reason in the writer's words, or it is not written.

## The amendment rule

**A post changed after it was approved goes through this pass again.**

This includes a post already sitting in a scheduler. The facts moving, a date
changing, a link resolving, all of it. An amendment is the moment a post gets
assembled out of parts, so it is exactly the moment the first test matters, and
it is the one moment the pass is easiest to skip because the post was approved
once already.

An edit to a scheduled post is a new post that happens to be replacing one.

## What this file does not do

Mechanics. Em dashes, house spelling, banned vocabulary, AI tells, unsourced
claims, character limits: all of that is `style-rules.md` and the channel table
in `social-schedule.md`. Run those too. A post has to pass both.

Reading ease and grade level are not measured on posts. They are calibrated on
running prose and a five-line post scores noise.

## Findings, and who decides

Same doctrine as every other edit stage in Familiar. Nothing is auto-applied,
nothing is fixed quietly, and "clean" is not something the pass may conclude
about its own work without showing what it checked.

For a mechanical finding, the format is `style-rules.md`'s: the quoted text, the
rule, one line on why it matters, and the exact rewrite. For a failure of tests
1 to 4, the format is the pick above.

The writer accepts, rejects or revises each one.
